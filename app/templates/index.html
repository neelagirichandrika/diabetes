Supervised Learning — Linear Models at Depth

Normal equations vs Gradient Descent, geometric L1 vs L2 regularization, Logistic sigmoid loss, and clinical Odds Ratio interpretation.

View Odds Ratios
Runnable Code: regularization/run_regularization.py • coefficients/run_coefficients.py
Terminal: py regularization/run_regularization.py

1. Normal Equations vs. Gradient Descent

Closed-form: θ* = (XTX)-1XTy • O(d3) matrix inversion.
Iterative: θ ← θ - (α/n) XT(Xθ - y) • Scales to millions of features.

2. Why L1 Induces Sparsity (Geometry)

L1 constraint forms a diamond with sharp corners on axes; expanding loss contours touch corners first, forcing non-predictive weights to exact zero. L2 constraint is smooth and spherical, shrinking weights without zeroes.

3. Logistic Sigmoid & Log-Loss Gradient

Sigmoid: p = σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e-z)
Loss: L = -(1/n) Σ [y ln(p) + (1-y) ln(1-p)]
Gradient: ∇wL = (1/n) XT(p - y)

4. Clinical Odds Ratios: Exp(wj)

Prior Inpatient (w = +0.3421) → OR = 1.408 (+40.8% odds)
Emergency Visits (w = +0.0984) → OR = 1.103 (+10.3% odds)
Prior utilization is the primary clinical driver of readmission.

Supervised Learning — Tree-Based Models

CART decision trees, pruning & depth overfitting, Random Forest variance reduction, Gradient Boosting, and HistGradientBoosting.

View Depth Overfitting
Runnable Code: decision_tree/run_decision_tree.py • splitting_criteria/run_splitting_criteria.py
Terminal: py decision_tree/run_decision_tree.py

1. CART Splitting Criteria

Gini Impurity: IGini = 1 - Σ pk2 = 2 p0 p1
Entropy: IEntropy = -Σ pk log2(pk)
Both yield nearly identical optimal split thresholds on clinical continuous counts.

2. Random Forest Variance Reduction Proof

Var(Forest) = ρ σ2 + [(1-ρ)/B] σ2 → ρ σ2
Random feature subsampling (√d) decorrelates trees (ρ → 0), driving total ensemble variance down.

3. Tree Depth Overfitting Dynamics

Depth 6: Train 62.8% | Test 62.3% | AUC 0.658 (Optimal)
Depth 20: Train 85.9% | Test 55.4% | AUC 0.562 (Severe Overfit)
Unconstrained deep trees memorize individual patient noise.

4. HistGradientBoosting (Production Workhorse)

256-bin continuous histogram discretization drops sort complexity from O(n log n) to O(n).
Production Metrics: 63.69% Accuracy | 0.6857 ROC-AUC

Unsupervised Learning — Patient Phenotyping & Anomaly Detection

Lloyd's K-Means, K-Means++, Hierarchical linkage dendrograms, DBSCAN density metrics, PCA standardization pitfalls, and cluster feature enrichment.

View Correlations
Runnable Code: eda/run_eda.py • src/preprocessing.py
Terminal: py eda/run_eda.py

1. K-Means Lloyd's Algorithm & WCSS

Minimizes Inertia: J = Σ Σ ||x - μk||2
Assignment: c(i) = argmin ||x(i) - μk||2
Update: μk = (1/|Ck|) Σ x

2. K-Means++ & Silhouette Score

K-Means++ samples initial centroids with probability proportional to D(x)2, providing an O(log K) bound.
Silhouette: s(i) = [b(i) - a(i)] / max(a(i), b(i)) ∈ [-1, 1]

3. Hierarchical Linkage & DBSCAN

Ward's Linkage minimizes within-cluster variance increase upon merging.
DBSCAN uses core (≥minPts within ε) and border points, flagging extreme clinical utilization as noise (label -1).

4. PCA Production Pitfall

Running PCA without standardization causes high-variance columns (lab procedures) to dominate components, completely erasing the signal of low-range predictors (prior inpatient admissions).

1. Feature Stores & Point-in-Time Correctness

Feast feature store decouples offline Parquet/Snowflake storage (for training) from online Redis storage (for <5ms serving), enforcing point-in-time joins ("time travel") to eliminate lookahead feature leakage.

2. Training-Serving Skew Elimination

Packaging the fitted ColumnTransformer and HistGradientBoostingClassifier into a single serialized diabetes_pipeline.joblib guarantees identical matrix transformations in training and production.

3. Containerized Packaging (Docker & Gunicorn)

Enforces reproducible Linux container runtime with python:3.11-slim, non-root user appuser for HIPAA clinical security, Gunicorn multi-worker WSGI, and automated /health probes.

4. Production Drift Monitoring: PSI Formula

PSI = Σ (Pprod - Ptrain) × ln(Pprod / Ptrain)
PSI < 0.10: Stable • 0.10 ≤ PSI < 0.25: Moderate shift warning • PSI ≥ 0.25: Trigger automated retraining pipeline.

1. Readmission Distribution

Actual distribution of inpatient readmission outcomes across raw classes: NO (54,864 encounters; 53.91%), >30 days (35,545 encounters; 34.93%), and <30 days (11,357 encounters; 11.16%). Under our binary formulation, any subsequent readmission comprises 46.09% of records versus 53.91% baseline.

2. Readmission Across Age Groups

Comparison of readmission distribution across 10 age cohorts. Inpatient volume peaks heavily in the [70-80) age cohort (26,068 encounters), while readmission likelihood rises progressively with patient age, reaching 48.27% in the [80-90) cohort.

3. Hospital Utilization

Distribution of core hospital resource utilization variables: length of stay (mean 4.40 days, median 4 days), diagnostic lab procedures (mean 43.10 tests, median 44 tests), and distinct medications prescribed (mean 16.02, median 15 medications).

4. Previous Hospital Visits and Readmission

Prior 12-month healthcare encounters represent the strongest clinical signals for future readmission. Patients readmitted within 30 days averaged 1.22 prior inpatient admissions (+220.5% higher than non-readmitted patients) and 0.36 emergency visits (+227.2% higher).

5. Numerical Feature Correlation

Correlation matrix across 8 meaningful clinical numerical measurements (excluding database IDs). Identifies strong positive association between number of medications administered and length of stay (r = 0.47), and prior emergency and inpatient utilization (r = 0.27).
